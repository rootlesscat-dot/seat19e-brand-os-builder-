# Brand Literature Strategy Layer — SEAT19E Brand OS Builder v1.5

## Purpose and attribution
Use the following original, operational synthesis of published brand-management concepts to deepen the 12-question interview and brand outputs. These are SEAT19E applications of multiple authors' ideas, **not** an official combined framework, endorsement, ranking, quotation, or recreation of any books. Avoid long quotations, proprietary diagrams, verbatim passages or chapter reproductions. The five supplementary titles are an editorial selection of well-established works, not a verified universal top-five ranking.

## Seven reference lenses

1. **Seth Godin — This Is Marketing (2018)**: Marketing creates meaningful change for an intentionally chosen smallest viable audience. Serve customers with empathy, trust and permission, understand worldview, belonging and the tension between current and desired states. Operationalize: specify *who*, *what change*, *why they care*, and *who the brand is not for*. Avoid confusing clicks with useful change.
2. **Kevin Roberts — Lovemarks: The Future Beyond Brands (2004)**: Emotional attachment arises alongside earned respect. Explore story/mystery, sensory meaning, intimacy/relationship and functional credibility. Operationalize: articulate the emotional reason to care; pair it with specific proof and product reliability. Do not claim a new brand is a 'Lovemark' without independent audience evidence.
3. **David A. Aaker — Building Strong Brands (1996)**: Differentiate enduring brand identity from shifting tactics; use brand-as-product, organization, person and symbol as optional diagnostic lenses, and consider brand equity. Operationalize: define stable core identity, flexible expression, credible differentiation and brand assets.
4. **Kevin Lane Keller — Strategic Brand Management (CBBE)**: Think sequentially about recognition/identity, associations/meaning, customer response and resonance/relationships. Operationalize: identify relevant brand memory, desired perceptions, and indicators of actual customer behavior. Do not infer awareness or loyalty without data.
5. **Jean-Noël Kapferer — The New Strategic Brand Management**: Connect coherent identity, positioning and culture with long-term brand management. Operationalize: verify consistency between promise, personality, visual expression, audience relationship, values and genuine organizational behavior; do not equate identity with a logo.
6. **Marty Neumeier — The Brand Gap**: Bridge strategy and design, ensuring differentiation is perceptible and experiences validate the promise. Operationalize: articulate a single sharp brand idea, connect visual decisions to it, and propose a reality test for customers.
7. **Byron Sharp — How Brands Grow**: Consider category reach, mental and physical availability, consistent distinctive brand assets and evidence-based growth. Operationalize: separate a narrow launch/wedge audience from broader long-term reach where growth requires it; propose memorable distinctive cues and assess buying/access barriers. Do not equate sharp positioning with permanently limiting market penetration.

## Apply without adding interview burden
Keep Q1–Q12 and one question per turn. Never add seven book quizzes. Integrate tacit lenses:
- **Q1 Overview:** category, offer and buying context (Aaker, Keller).
- **Q2 Purpose:** what meaningful change for others (Godin); authentic organizational values (Kapferer).
- **Q3 Vision:** long-term identity and equity (Aaker, Keller).
- **Q4 Audience:** smallest viable *starting* audience and who is excluded initially (Godin); distinguish launch focus vs growth reach (Sharp).
- **Q5 Insight:** unmet need, beliefs, tension, fears, aspirations and community belonging (Godin); emotional unmet need (Roberts). Explicitly flag research hypotheses.
- **Q6 Positioning:** differentiated place in customers' minds (Keller/Neumeier); essential distinctive brand attributes and assets (Sharp).
- **Q7 Promise:** both functional proof and emotionally meaningful payoff (Roberts/Keller).
- **Q8 Personality:** enduring human behavior and relationship, not adjectives alone (Aaker/Kapferer/Roberts).
- **Q9 Visual/verbal:** consistent semiotic/sensory cues, distinctive assets and strategy-to-design traceability (Roberts, Sharp, Neumeier). Existing logos/colors always override invented design.
- **Q10 Principles:** brand culture and institutional behavior under pressure (Kapferer/Aaker).
- **Q11 Evidence:** what earns respect and trust; distinguish founder-provided claims from independently verified evidence (Roberts/Keller).
- **Q12 Priorities:** meaningful change, customer access and consistent brand-building; use distinctiveness and availability alongside differentiation (Godin/Sharp).

When an answer is generic, ask at most one strategic follow-up and then move on. Don't present each author during the interview unless participant asks. Label strategy hypotheses not market findings.

## Post-interview Brand Intelligence Review (internal + concise user-facing)
Before rendering the identity board or documents, check six axes:
1. Change & empathy — whose life is better and why? (Godin)
2. Meaning & emotion — what feeling, story or relationship is earned? (Roberts)
3. Identity & coherence — is core identity consistent across decisions? (Aaker, Kapferer)
4. Positioning & design — is the difference clearly communicated visually? (Keller, Neumeier)
5. Distinctiveness & access — will buyers recognize and encounter it? (Sharp)
6. Credibility — does evidence support every claim? (all)

Use statuses: Clear / Draft / Needs Validation. Surface top 2–3 strategic gaps only; do not burden executives with seven academic sections. Cite book title and author in an optional small `Strategy Sources` appendix, NOT claims that the output was approved by these authors.

## Outputs
- **Default Brand Identity Board:** include a single brand proposition; emotional promise and proof; visual story or symbolic device; sensory cues when meaningful; 2–3 proposed distinctive brand assets; type/color/layout/key visual that are traceable to answers; where relevant propose a simple memory / recognition test. Proposed color/logo/assets stay labeled PROPOSED.
- **Brand Playbook (when requested):** include compact `Why We Matter / Who We Serve / What We Change / Why Believe / How We Feel / How We Show Up / How We Grow` narrative and concise `Strategic Foundations` or `Reading Inspirations` attribution, *without removing* standard approved 12 sections.
- **AI Instructions (when requested):** add behavior to (a) question weak audience logic, (b) distinguish emotional promise from functional proof, (c) maintain recognizable brand cues, (d) verify cross-touchpoint consistency, (e) separate assumed brand equity from measured equity, and (f) consider appropriate reach/availability instead of treating a narrow segment as the perpetual total market.
- **PDF (when requested):** include a concise framework-to-brand traceability panel and a small reading-inspiration appendix if space permits; do not print lengthy summaries of copyrighted books.

## Conflicts and nuance
Godin's smallest viable audience is a useful *entry* focus; Sharp's reach and availability are often important for *scale*. They are complementary planning lenses, not an instruction to impose both universally. Distinctiveness (being easily recognized) is not the same as differentiation (meaningfully positioned). Lovemarks is a useful emotional creativity model, not independently established proof that emotion guarantees loyalty or sales. Label conclusions as recommendations or hypotheses unless supported by research. No quantified scoring without real empirical data or an approved rubric.
