## Six design books and application rules

1. **Designing Brand Identity (6th ed., 2024), Alina Wheeler and Rob Meyerson.** Branding is a process from research and strategy to identity system, application and governance. Apply this to identity architecture: promise, name/wordmark direction, palette, typography, imagery, digital/physical touchpoints, consistency. Ask: Can the visual grammar scale across at least two channels? Avoid a one-off attractive poster with no system. Official publisher: Wiley, ISBN 9781119984818.

2. **Creating a Brand Identity: A Guide for Designers, Catharine Slade-Brooking.** The user called this “Brand Design”; the evidenced English title is *Creating a Brand Identity*. Research and analysis precede concept development, visual research boards and final communication boards. Require audience and category considerations, concept statement, differentiation and an explicit link from raw references to own design choices. Distinguish observation from hypothesis; do not imitate references. Bibliographic TOC confirms research boards, mood board exercises, competitor analysis and presentation boards.

3. **Decorate Workshop: Design and Style Your Space in 8 Creative Steps, Holly Becker.** Transfer moodboard thinking from spatial styling into brand sensory coherence: texture, scale, materials, palette, ambiance and deliberate editing. This is not a substitute for business positioning. Translate tactile signals to packaging, photography surface, backdrop and screen-safe treatments. Label as cross-disciplinary inspiration, not strict brand-design authority.

4. **Basics Fashion Design 01: Research and Design, Simon Seivewright.** Use inspiration research, concept/narrative development, collage and iterative editing to convert diverse sources into an original creative territory. Establish a unifying visual storyline. Connect imagery, human subjects, materiality and movement. Avoid presenting an arbitrary Pinterest-like collage as a finished system.

## Mandatory design bridge (after discovery)
Create an internal strategy-to-visual matrix with six fields: (1) audience insight, (2) brand value/personality, (3) visual metaphor/idea, (4) palette/texture/photo art direction, (5) grid/type/logo expression, (6) real touchpoint proof. If values conflict, show trade-offs. Check 2–3 distinctly different directions internally; select the one best aligned to strategic constraints; avoid submitting three incoherent mini-brands as one board. Existing approved design assets override speculative replacements.

## Moodboard vs Identity Board
Moodboard = inspirational, associative, research-stage visual territory with atmosphere, references and sensory cues; clearly mark concepts and reference status. Identity Board = prescriptive proposed/approved design grammar: brand promise, wordmark direction, color system with HEX, typographic hierarchy, imagery style, distinctive devices and 2 practical mockups. A final board can contain a small moodboard panel but must visibly distinguish inspiration from adopted/proposed brand elements.

## Creative direction variables
- **Color**: propose 4–6 swatches, HEX exact in copy; show usage proportions and color contrast concerns; do not claim trademark uniqueness.
- **Typography**: heading/body hierarchy, scale, weight, kerning/tracking cues, Korean/English legibility, accessibility; avoid naming a proprietary font as adopted without proof.
- **Composition**: establish alignment, grid rhythm, deliberate whitespace, focal hierarchy and crop behavior; demonstrate responsive or packaging adaptability.
- **Imagery**: articulate photography vs illustration, lighting, subject, authenticity, lens/crop feel, mood, culturally respectful depiction.
- **Material and texture**: print/digital applications, tactile cues, restraint, category appropriateness.
- **Brand graphic assets**: propose memorable motif or shape derived from distinctive idea, not random decoration.
- **Applications**: two industry-relevant examples, plausible placeholders for unknown metrics and product claims.

## Quality review checklist (7-pass)
1. Each key visual choice ties to purpose, positioning, promise or personality.
2. Distinctive memory cues that do not rely on category clichés.
3. Coherent mood across hero art, palette, type, textures and mockups.
4. Readable hierarchy and sufficient negative space; note contrast or small-type failures.
5. Versatile across two real brand touchpoints.
6. Feasible with proposed assets, budget/material capabilities; flag unknowns.
7. No fabricated claims, copied protected designs, misleading approvals or invented research.

## Output addition
Alongside the image and concise strategy summary, provide a brief **Visual Design Rationale**: brand promise → selected visual territory → why these colors/type/imagery/layout convey it; identify proposed vs approved assets, and one most valuable next validation. In a PDF place this explanation on a separate page or sidebar; never clutter the board.
